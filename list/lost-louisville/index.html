Lost Louisville

Gone but not forgotten. A memorial wall for the places we still give directions by.

Open the interactive version →
  • Mazzoni's (Est. 1884–c.2010) · Google ↗

    Started at Third and Market, handing out a free rolled oyster with every beer. The family's last restaurant closed around 2010.

  • Fontaine Ferry Park (Est. 1905–1969) · Google ↗

    A riverside amusement park in the West End with 50+ rides, a pool and a skating rink. It barred Black visitors until 1964, and it closed after unrest on opening day in 1969.

  • Kentucky Colonels (Est. 1967–1976) · Google ↗

    Louisville's ABA basketball team. They won the 1975 championship, then folded when the ABA merged with the NBA.

  • Casa Grisanti (Est. until 1991) · Google ↗

    The Grisanti family's white-tablecloth Italian room. Its alumni went on to run Vincenzo's, Corbett's and the English Grill, and Ditto's and Stevens & Stevens grew out of the same group.

  • The Toy Tiger (Est. 1973–1999) · Google ↗

    Rock club at Bardstown Rd and Goldsmith Ln, with touring acts like GWAR and Queens of the Stone Age, wild contests and that neon sign. Torn down for a gas station.

  • Deitrich's (Est. 1988–2003) · Google ↗

    Brasserie Deitrich's filled the old Crescent Theatre on Frankfort Ave (locals joked about dinner at the blue movies). It and Allo Spiedo helped turn Frankfort Ave into a restaurant row.

  • Kaelin's (Est. 1934–2009) · Google ↗

    The Newburg Road restaurant that claimed the first cheeseburger. The building now serves burgers as 80/20 at Kaelin's.

  • ear X-tacy (Est. 1985–2011) · Google ↗

    The Bardstown Road record store that defined Louisville music for 25 years. Its sign now hangs in the Frazier History Museum.

  • Lynn's Paradise Cafe (Est. 1991–2013) · Google ↗

    The kitschy Barret Ave brunch spot with the giant coffee pot out front and the Ugly Lamp Contest at the State Fair.

  • Phoenix Hill Tavern (Est. 1976–2015) · Google ↗

    The sprawling Baxter Ave music club. Closed abruptly in 2015 and torn down in 2017. Its moose head went to the Frazier.

  • Baxter Avenue Theatres (Est. 1996–2025) · Google ↗

    The Highlands' indie and midnight-movie house. It closed Dec. 31, 2025.

  • Rauch Planetarium (Est. 2001–2026) · Google ↗

    UofL's dome went dark in 2020 and never reopened. The university made the closure permanent in March 2026.
