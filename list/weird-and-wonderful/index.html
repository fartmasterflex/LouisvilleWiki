Weird & Wonderful

Free oddities, giant bats, cursed trees and famous graves, plus the best sunsets, star parties and sledding hills. The sledding hills open once 2 to 3 inches stick: no sledding on golf courses, no bonfires.

Legends & Curiosities

  • The Witches' Tree (Old Louisville) · free · Search ↗

    The gnarled tree at the northwest corner of 6th & Park. Legend says witches cursed the city when their maple was cut for an 1889 May Day maypole, and the 1890 tornado followed. Locals still leave it offerings.

  • Pope Lick Trestle (Fisherville) · free · Search ↗

    Home of the Pope Lick Monster, Louisville's half-man, half-goat. Look from the ground ONLY. It's an active rail line and people have died on that trestle.

  • Hunter S. Thompson's Boyhood Home (Cherokee Triangle) · free · Search ↗

    2437 Ransdell Ave, where the inventor of gonzo journalism grew up. It's a private home, so wave from the sidewalk.

  • Gallopalooza Horses · free · Search ↗

    Life-size painted horse statues that pop up on sidewalks and lawns around town. The program started in 2004 and comes back for big Derby years.

Giant Things

  • The World's Largest Bat (Downtown) · free · Search ↗

    The 120-foot steel Slugger leaning on the Louisville Slugger Museum on Main St.

  • Caufield's Vampire Bat (Downtown) · free · Search ↗

    A 36-foot bat (the flying kind) clinging to Caufield's Novelty at 10th & Main. Built in 2000 as a prank on the baseball bat down the street.

Statues & Graves

  • The Thinker (UofL Belknap) · free · Search ↗

    In front of Grawemeyer Hall since 1949. UofL says it's the first large-scale bronze cast of Rodin's Thinker, made under Rodin's eye in 1904.

  • Daniel Boone Statue (Cherokee Park) · free · Search ↗

    Enid Yandell's bronze frontiersman at the Eastern Parkway end of the park, cast in 1906. It survived the 1974 tornado.

  • Lincoln Memorial at Waterfront Park (Waterfront) · free · Search ↗

    A larger-than-life Lincoln on a rock, looking out at the river, by Louisville sculptor Ed Hamilton (2009).

  • Cave Hill's Famous Graves (Cave Hill) · free · Search ↗

    Colonel Sanders, Muhammad Ali, George Rogers Clark, Pappy Van Winkle and Patty and Mildred Hill, who wrote the 'Happy Birthday' tune. Grab a free map at the office.

Golden Hour & Night Sky

  • Iroquois Park North Overlook (Iroquois Park) · free · Search ↗

    What some call the best view of downtown. Cars can drive up Wed, Sat and Sun from April to October, 10 AM to 8 PM. Otherwise walk or bike up and plan on coming down in the dark.

  • Big Four Bridge at Sunset (Waterfront) · free · Search ↗

    Walk out over the Ohio for golden hour, then stay for the LED light show on the bridge after dark.

  • Big Four Station Park (Jeffersonville, IN) · free · Search ↗

    The best skyline view of Louisville is from Indiana. Walk across the bridge and look back.

  • Shawnee Park Riverbank (West End) · free · Search ↗

    The sun sets right over the Ohio from Olmsted's riverside park.

  • Louisville Astronomical Society Star Parties (Tom Sawyer State Park) · free · Site ↗

    Free public star parties one Saturday a month, March to November, at the Urban Astronomy Center in Tom Sawyer Park or the dark-sky Baker Observatory in Curby, IN (about an hour). Weather decides, so check their Facebook first.

Snow Day Sledding Hills

  • Dog Hill (Cherokee Park) (Cherokee Park) · free · Search ↗

    Baringer Hill, better known as Dog Hill, at Alexander Rd and Scenic Loop. The classic.

  • Joe Creason Park (Trevilian Way) · free · Search ↗

    Across from the Zoo. Some sledders swear it beats Dog Hill.

  • George Rogers Clark Park (Thruston Ave) · free · Search ↗

    1024 Thruston Ave, behind the playground near McKinley Ave.

  • McNeely Lake Park (Cooper Chapel Rd) · free · Search ↗

    Near the Korean War Memorial.
