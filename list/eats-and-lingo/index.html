Only-in-Louisville Eats

  • Hot Brown · Search ↗

    Open-faced turkey and bacon drowned in Mornay sauce and broiled. Invented at the Brown Hotel in 1926. Get the original at the Brown.

  • Benedictine · Search ↗

    A pale-green cucumber and cream cheese spread created by Louisville caterer Jennie Benedict around 1900. Look for it on sandwiches at delis, tea rooms and Derby parties.

  • Modjeska · Search ↗

    A marshmallow dipped in caramel, named in 1883 for the Polish actress Helena Modjeska. Muth's Candies on East Market still makes them by hand.

  • Rolled Oysters · Search ↗

    Three oysters battered, rolled in cracker meal and fried into one fist-sized ball. Mazzoni's (1884) gets the credit. Try them at Check's Cafe, KingFish, Hungry Pelican or Mike Linnig's.

  • Henry Bain Sauce · Search ↗

    A sweet-and-tangy steak sauce dreamed up by Henry Bain, the Pendennis Club's maître d'. It's still sold by the bottle around town.

  • The Old Fashioned · Search ↗

    The Pendennis Club claims the cocktail was born there in the 1880s. Other cities argue. We don't listen.

  • Mint Julep · Search ↗

    Bourbon, mint, sugar and crushed ice. The official drink of the Kentucky Derby. Most locals drink exactly one a year.

  • That Chocolate-Walnut Pie · Search ↗

    Derby-Pie® is a registered trademark of Kern's Kitchen, a Louisville family business that has defended the name for decades. Everyone else bakes a 'Derby Day pie' and winks.

  • Bourbon Balls · Search ↗

    Bourbon-spiked candy invented in 1938 by Rebecca Ruth Candy in Frankfort. Every candy shop in town sells its own.

  • Burgoo · Search ↗

    A slow-cooked stew of whatever meat and vegetables are around, made by the vat. More of a western Kentucky thing, but it turns up at Derby parties and church picnics.

  • The Cheeseburger (Allegedly) · Search ↗

    Kaelin's on Newburg Road claimed to have invented it in 1934. The building serves burgers again as 80/20 at Kaelin's.

  • Church Fish Sandwich · Search ↗

    A slab of fried cod three times the size of its bun, eaten at a Lenten fish fry with a side of hushpuppies. See the Church Picnics & Fish Fries list.

  • Spaghetti With Chili On It · Search ↗

    That's Cincinnati. We don't talk about Cincinnati.

The Lingo

  • "What high school did you go to?" · Search ↗

    The first question anyone asks you here. It isn't small talk. It tells them your neighborhood, your rivals, and which of your cousins they already know.

  • The Watterson · Search ↗

    I-264, the inner loop. Named for Henry Watterson, the Courier-Journal's famous editor. Usually said with a sigh.

  • The Snyder · Search ↗

    I-265, the outer loop (the Gene Snyder Freeway). If someone lives 'out past the Snyder', they have a big yard.

  • Spaghetti Junction · Search ↗

    The Kennedy Interchange downtown, where I-64, I-65 and I-71 tangle together by the river.

  • Thunder · Search ↗

    As in 'You going to Thunder?' Thunder Over Louisville, the giant riverfront fireworks show that kicks off the Derby Festival.

  • Oaks Day · Search ↗

    The Friday before the Derby. The locals' day at the track, and plenty of offices and schools treat it as a holiday.

  • Cards or Cats · Search ↗

    UofL Cardinals or UK Wildcats. Every household picks a side. Mixed marriages exist but are discussed in hushed tones.

  • The Dainty · Search ↗

    The World Championship Dainty Contest, a street game in Schnitzelburg where you whack a small stick as far as it will go. Every July.

  • The 502 · Search ↗

    The area code, worn on T-shirts, tattoos and bumper stickers as a personality.

  • Across the River · Search ↗

    Southern Indiana: Jeffersonville, New Albany, Clarksville. Close enough to commute, far enough to joke about.

  • Keep Louisville Weird · Search ↗

    The rallying cry for independent local businesses, seen on bumper stickers all over the Highlands.
